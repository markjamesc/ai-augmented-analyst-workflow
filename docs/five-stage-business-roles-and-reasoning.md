# Five-Stage Workflow: Business Roles, Reasoning Today, and Future Ambitions

The workflow mirrors a common division between executive decisions and analytical work, while using reasoning throughout.

The role column describes a common traditional mapping, not a universal staffing rule. This document is explanatory and aspirational; it does not change current procedures, gates, or approval authority, and the owner continues to set the goals the workflow serves.

| Stage | Traditional responsibility (common mapping) | Reasoning today | Future ambition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Decision** | Executives set the goal and decision. | Clarify objectives, actions, constraints, and what matters. | Independently assess which decision deserves attention, rather than only clarify the executive's request. |
| **2. Framing** | Executives, supported by analysts, define the question. | Turn the decision into an answerable analytical question. | Reason about which question would best support the decision and challenge a mistaken framing. |
| **3. Design** | Analysts design the analysis. | Choose hypotheses, metrics, methods, assumptions, and checks. | Strengthen mathematical and statistical justification, compare alternative designs, and recognize when better data or an experiment is needed. |
| **4. Execution** | Analysts retrieve data and perform calculations. | Use SQL to retrieve and prepare database inputs, then independent R-A/R-B builds to perform and cross-check the analysis, including machine learning where appropriate. | Retain the current approach; make targeted improvements only when testing reveals a need. |
| **5. Interpretation** | Analysts explain findings; executives decide. | Assess what the results support, acknowledge uncertainty, and recommend a justified action. | Strengthen reasoning about alternative explanations, causal limits, benefits, costs, and risks—including when not to act. |

The ambition is **stronger reasoning, not merely more automation**. These are future directions, not capabilities already established by testing. Stage 4 is well-tested, not perfected; accuracy testing must assess whether the workflow produces correct analysis and defensible recommendations.
